# All teams summary

Thailand in 2019 and Vietnam and Philippines in 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup played nine matches between them, in which only 1 game ended up being a win and the other 8 were all losses, with 2 goals scored and 40 conceded.

The question throughout the project is how heavy defeats could have been avoided, in which the short answer is that most goals came from restarts and turnovers, which are moments that teams could have a hand in, but those goals mostly made defeats heavier than deciding them. The results came down to gap in quality and how little the three teams created offensively.

## How the goals came

Of the 40 goals, 22 came from restarts and turnovers, 7 from low-quality chances, and 11 from the quality gap.

The same pattern held for all three teams, as restarts and turnovers made up the largest share for Thailand (11 of 20), Vietnam (7 of 12), and the Philippines (4 of 8).

## Heavier defeats, not decided results

However, for 8 games where these three teams lost, only in 3 games were the opening goals came from restarts and turnovers, which were a Sweden free kick after 6 minutes against Thailand, a Vietnam loss near the halfway line against Portugal after 7 minutes, and a Switzerland penalty against the Philippines just before halftime. The other 5 opening goals were from the quality gap.

Among 22 goals conceded from restarts and turnovers, 12 of them came when the gap was already three or more. This can be seen in Thailand's 13-0 loss against USWNT, with USWNT leading by 3 at half time, and then they scored 4 more in the 6 minutes after the break, with 3 of them coming from Thailand losing the ball in their own half or conceding a free kick. The goals did not decide the losing result, but turned a heavy defeat into a record one.

## The main mechanism: Losing the ball in their own half

The main route to conceding goals was losing the ball in their own half, which happened in 13 out of 40 goals (6 for Thailand, 5 for Vietnam, and 2 for the Philippines), followed by set pieces and throw-ins with 7, then penalties and lost goal kicks with 3 each.

How often it happened was mostly a team trait. Thailand and Vietnam lost the ball in their own half around 30 times a match regardless of their opponent, with 42 for Vietnam against the Netherlands' high press being the peak. On the other hand, the Philippines did it about a third as often, although their opponents pressed less high. How often those losses were punished depended more on the opponent, as in Vietnam's case, Portugal turned far more of Vietnam's own-half losses into shots than USWNT did despite not pressing as high, as they attacked more quickly after winning the ball.

The same story can also be observed with goal kicks, as Thailand and Vietnam kept the ball for 10 seconds after only about 1 in 4 times, while the Philippines went long with every goal kick and kept the ball after about 4 in 10.

## Luck

Luck is also another factor making heavy defeats heavier but not chaning any result. In the three haviest defeats, far more went in than the chances suggested. In most of the other matches, fewer went in. With SEA teams created little at the other end, no result was decided by luck.

## The shared weakness: Attack

Each team only had 10 to 13 shots across their 3 matches, worth about half a goal in xG altogether. Only 2 goals were scored, with Kanjana Sungngoen's goal off the counter against Sweden and Sarina Bolden's game-winning header against New Zealand.

## Three teams, three stories

**Thailand 2019** conceded diferently in each match, with own-half losses against USWNT, restarts against Sweden, and a turnover and an own goal against Chile. Their numbers improved as the tournament went on, but their opponents' strength also fall. One clear change of approach was a much higher defensive line against Chile, which kept the match level until half-time.

**Vietnam 2023** passed the ball better in every match, but were punished more each time for turning the ball over in their own half, and their heaviest defeat came last against the Netherlands who pressed the highest among Vietnam's 3 opponents.

**The Philippines 2023** were the only team that had a clear and consistent approach in every game with every goal kick long, fewer losses near their own goal and a higher defensive line than Thailand's or Vietnam's, which kept them in matches and even got them 1 win against New Zealand, but they created little in attack.

## The Philippines towards 2027

**What to keep:**

- A safe build-up, including long goal kicks and losing less near their own goal limited the risk that cost Thailand and Vietnam so many goals
- A higher defensive line

**What to work on:**

- Turning attacks into chances. While the Philippines reached the final third more often than Thailand or Vietnam, they only converted around 1 in 12 of those possessions into shots, against roughly 1 in 6 for Thailand and Vietnam.
- Set pieces and the box. They conceded 2 penalties, and New Zealand's 3 best chances came from corners.
- Responding when behind. After conceding against Switzerland, the Philippines only shot once more, and against Norway the match was gone by half time.

## Limitations

- **Nine matches against different opponents.** Changes from one match to the next mix what a team did with how strong the opponent was.
- **Event data only.** Without 360 data, there is no information about positioning off the ball. Hence defending is judged only by what happened on the ball.
- **Goal types are rules.** Borderline cases include own goals, judged by how the move started, and goals that followed errors, such as USWNT's 3rd goal against Vietnam, which counts as quality gap.
- **xG does not measure finishing quality.** "More than the chances suggested" is not purely luck, as strong finshers regularly beat xG.
