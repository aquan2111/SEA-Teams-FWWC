# MD1: Philippines 0-2 Switzerland

**Story:** The Philippines defended more highly against Switzerland, and never lost the ball in their own third, but a penalty just before half-time and a three-shot Swiss attack at the 64th minute decided the game, while the Philippines only managed 3 shots.

## Context

This was the first match for both teams. Switzerland had their second appearance at a FIFA Women's World Cup, the first one in 2015 where they got to the round of 16. On the other hand, this is the Philippines' first ever FIFA Women's World Cup.

Switzerland ended up topping this group after drawing their next two games, before suffering a 5-1 defeat to the eventual champion Spain in the round of 16.

## Turning point

Just before half-time, Switzerland converted a penalty at the 45th minute. At the 64th minute, Switzerland picked up their 2nd goal, and then the Philippines only had 1 other shot.

## Goals conceded

While the 1st goal was a penalty, the 2nd goal was from a build up from Switzerland.

## Luck

Switzerland scored 1 goal off 2.57 non-penalty expected goals, but about 1.5 of that came from a single attack sequence at the 64th minute where Switzerland needed 3 shots in 3 seconds to get their second goal. However, apart from that, Switzerland still created more than they scored, so the Philippines were somewhat fortunate to not concede earlier.

Apart from that attack sequence at the 64th minute, most of Switzerland's clearer chances happened before the 1st goal, with the best chance coming from a close shot by Ana-Maria Crnogorcevic going over at the 36th minute, so the Philippines did well to stay level until the penalty.

## Biggest gap

The biggest gap can be seen in Switzerland having more final third entries and touches and box entries. In addition, Switzerland's possessions lasted longer with more completed passes.

## What went well

The Philippines rarely lost the ball in their own half and no losses were recorded in the Philippines' own third.

## Narrative

The Philippines were more willing to engage defensively more highly, while Switzerland did not have their defensive line too much higher. At the 15th minute, the Philippines put the ball into Switzerland's net, but the goal was ruled out for offside. After this, Switzerland had few more chances, notably around the 35th minute with a tap over the bar by Ana-Maria Crnogorcevic. Just before half time, they conceded a penalty late and Ramona Bachmann converted to give Switzerland the lead. At the 64th minute, coming from a build up of their own, Switzerland needed 3 attempts close to the Philippines' goal to finally get their 2nd goal. Both teams did not have any clear chances after.

## What could have been done

- Conceding a penalty late in the first half was costly, in which the defender came in for a loose ball but did not manage to get her foot in faster than the Swiss attacker.
- Despite not losing the ball in their own third, the Philippines struggled to create chances with only 3 shots, all low quality chances.
