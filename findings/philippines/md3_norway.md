# MD3: Philippines 0-6 Norway

**Story:** Norway, chasing a high margin win to secure qualification, started quickly and got 3 goals by the 31st minute and 5 goals by the 53rd minute. The Philippines went down to 10 at the 63rd minute, and Sophie Roman Haug completed her hat-trick in stoppage time.

## Context

This was the third match for both teams.

Having not started well, Norway needed to win to keep their chances alive. If Switzerland won, a win would have been enough for Norway. If Switzerland and New Zealand drew, Norway needed to win by at least 2 goals. If New Zealand won, Norway would have been on equal points with Switzerland, and the margin of the victories would decide who would go through.

The Philippines would have automatically been out if they lost this game. If they drew this game, they would need Switzerland to beat New Zealand to get through, while a win would have guaranteed a ticket to the round of 16 for the Philippines.

Norway ended their journey at the round of 16 where they got eliminated by Japan after a 3-1 loss.

## Turning point

Norway started strongly from early on, and they got their first goal at the 6th minute by Sophie Roman Haug. Haug scored once again at the 17th minute to make it 2-0, before another goal from Caroline Graham Hansen at the 31st made it 3-0 before half time. However, as Norway chased for more goals to ensure them advancing to the next round, they picked up two more goals early into the second half, before another goal from Haug during stoppage time to make it a hat-trick and make it 6-0 in the end. The Philippines picked up a red card at the 63rd minute; however, by that time, the game was essentially over with Philippines being down by 5.

## Goals conceded

The first 3 goals all came from open play, although all of them were rather difficult shots from tighter angles. The 4th goal was an own goal starting from the Philippines losing the ball in their own half, followed by a failed clearance that would otherwise be a likely goal for Guro Reiten at the far post coming from a low cross from the right from Graham Hansen. The 5th goal came from a penalty in which the Philippines' defenders tried to stop Reiten from making a close shot. The 6th goal happened after the Philippines lost the ball in their own half, Guro Reiten made a cross from the left flank, and Sophie Roman Haug converted a difficult header.

## Luck

The Philippines conceded 6 goals, including 1 own goal and 1 penalty, from 2.7 non-penalty expected goals, with all the 4 other goals coming from lower quality chances, suggesting that they did not deserve to concede as many goals in this game.

## Biggest gap

Similar to the previous two games, the biggest gap in this game can be seen in Norway having more final third entries and touches and box entries. Norway's possessions lasted longer with more completed passes. Norway also made almost 8 times as many shots as the Philippines.

## What went well

The Philippines had the highest proportion of their possessions reaching Norway's final third and box among their 3 games.

## Narrative

Norway needed a winning margin, so they came out on the front foot. By being clinical, Norway were able to convert some of their difficult chances to go up by 3 before half time. Such pressure did not stop as Norway picked up 2 more goals just when the 2nd half started through an own goal and a penalty. Things went from bad to worse for the Philippines as Sofia Harrison got a red card at the 63rd minute. One more goal by Sophie Roman Haug completed her hat-trick for the day and helped Norway finish second to get to the round of 16.

## What could have been done

- The Philippines struggled with creating chances in this game, even from set pieces and restarts.
- 3 of 6 goals came from restarts and turnovers, but they all came when Norway were already 3-0 up via 3 difficult finishes in the first half.
