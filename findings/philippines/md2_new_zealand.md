# MD2: Philippines 1-0 New Zealand

**Story:** The Philippines matched New Zealand's defensive height and kept them to poor chances from open play. Sarina Bolden headed in from a cross after a free kick with the Philippines' first shot, and New Zealand's best chances, mostly from corners, went unconverted.

## Context

This was the second match for both teams. Despite not entering the tournament with the best form, co-host New Zealand managed to beat the 1995 champion Norway 1-0 in the first game of the tournament. On the other hand, the Philippines lost 2-0 to the other European representative of the group - Switzerland.

Both teams ended up being eliminated from the group stage, as New Zealand drew 0-0 to Switzerland while the Philippines lost 6-0 to Norway.

## Turning point

At the 6th minute, coming from a corner, New Zealand got their first big chance but Indiah-Paige Riley's shot went wide. Following a free kick in the 24th minute, Sarina Bolden scored a header giving the Philippines the lead, which was their first shot of the game. After this, New Zealand kept on creating chances, especially in the second half mostly from set pieces, but could not capitalize.

## Chances conceded

The Philippines kept a clean sheet in this game.

New Zealand's clearest chances came from direct set piece situations, but most of them were not too clear as the one with the highest expected goal of 0.2 was a header just outside the six-yard box going slightly over by Hannah Wilkinson at the 58th minute. Their open play chances were mostly low-quality.

## Luck

The Philippines only had 4 shots accumulating 0.31 expected goals, with their goal having an expected goal of 0.17, which suggested that the Philippines were clinical in their first and only clear chance they had throughout the game.

New Zealand had 18 shots worth 1.36 expected goals but could not score any, including Jacqui Hand hitting the post at the 64th minute and having a goal cancelled at the 68th minute when Hannah Wilkinson was offside by just her head and her shoulder. On the chances alone, a draw would have not been unfair.

## Biggest gap

Similar to the Switzerland game, the biggest gap in this game can be seen in New Zealand having more final third entries and touches and box entries. In addition, New Zealand's possessions lasted longer with more completed passes.

## What went well

In their first attempt of the game, the Philippines managed to capitalize, which was their only goal of the game.

The Philippines' defensive distance was the same as New Zealand's, and after scoring, the Philippines were more active in pressing and had more of the ball against New Zealand.

For New Zealand's open play shots, the Philippines managed to keep these shots far or if they were in the box, they would not be too clear. Most of New Zealand's chances came from set pieces.

## Narrative

New Zealand started on the front foot, and Indiah-Paige Riley went close from a corner at the 6th minute. Against the run of play, at the 24th minute, coming from a cross on the right flank, Sarina Bolden scored the opener for the Philippines, which was their first shot of the game. New Zealand had most of their clearer chances coming from set pieces but could not convert. At the 68th minute, New Zealand's Jacqui Hand managed to score, but the goal was ruled out for Hannah Wilkinson being offside. The Philippines ended up with their first ever win in a FIFA Women's World Cup in their second ever game.

## What could have been done

- Set pieces were New Zealand's main route to goal, including their three best chances. Even though nothing went in, such situations were still risks that needed to be investigated.
- Creating chances from open play was still the gap.
